The Scientist Who Inspired a Nation

Scientist, teacher, author and the 11th President of India, remembered for his contribution to science and his connection with the dreams of young people.

01

A Life of Purpose

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who became the 11th President of India. Born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he developed a strong interest in science and engineering. He studied Aeronautical Engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology and later built a distinguished career in India's scientific institutions.

During his work with ISRO, Dr. Kalam made important contributions to India's space programme. As Project Director of the SLV-III programme, he played a leading role in the successful mission that placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in July 1980.

Dr. Kalam later worked with the Defence Research and Development Organisation, where he contributed to India's guided missile development programme. He held important scientific responsibilities and became closely associated with India's development of indigenous defence technology.

In 2002, Dr. Kalam became the 11th President of India and served until 2007. After his presidency, he continued teaching, writing and interacting with students. He received the Bharat Ratna in 1997 and remains remembered for connecting science, education and the aspirations of young people.

03

A Lasting Legacy

Dr. Kalam's life continues to inspire students and young people across India. His journey from a small town to India's highest constitutional office demonstrated the importance of education, perseverance and purposeful work.

His contribution went beyond science and technology. Through his books, lectures and interactions with students, he encouraged young people to think about the future and contribute to the development of the nation.
